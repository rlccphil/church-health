At a glance

The agricultural leader’s checklist

Soil preparation: assess readiness and the relational climate before launching anything. Seed selection: clear, biblical, reproducible discipleship outcomes. Seasonality and patience: sustainable rhythms that respect God’s timing. Continuous cultivation and weeding: pastoral care, accountability, and pruning. Reciprocal yield: measure success by how many reproducers are trained, not just seating capacity.

1 Corinthians 3:6: we plant and water; God makes it grow.

How the evaluation works

Change % = (new − base) ÷ base × 100. An environment’s verdict uses the average change across the categories you filled in.

Declining: down by the threshold or more. Plateauing: no change, or change smaller than the threshold. Growing: up by the threshold or more. Reproducing: up 100% or more (the number doubled). Multiplying: growth that compounds, meaning growth on top of growth.

Measuring compounding: open “Add the earlier period” on any row and enter the number from the period before your base. If both periods grew by the threshold or more, the growth is compounding, and the verdict becomes Multiplying. The Rule of 72 then estimates doubling time: 72 ÷ growth rate per period. For example, 12% growth each quarter doubles in about 6 quarters.

Communities and ministries. Each community or ministry enters its own numbers. “View results for” shows the whole church (everyone added together) or a single community or ministry.

Ratios show the shape of the church, such as volunteers per 100 attendees or circle members per Circle Leader. Soul Health is a short 1 to 5 self-rating by each leader on soil, seed, roots, cultivation, and rhythms. Read it with the numbers: rising numbers with low soul health call for deepening, and flat numbers with strong soul health may be an underground season.

Small groups swing widely: going from 2 to 3 is +50%. Bases under 10 are marked “small base,” so read them with care.

Numbers are a tool for discernment, not a verdict on anyone’s faithfulness.
